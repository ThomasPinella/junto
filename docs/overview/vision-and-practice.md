# Vision and Practice

## The Junto practice

Each chapter is an independent, recurring, in-person essay and discussion group. Between meetings, every member writes an essay of roughly 750–3,500 words on a topic of their choosing. The form is intentionally broad: a personal experience, reflection on a value, historical deep dive, prediction, or thesis can all qualify. The important constraint is that the author takes a stance or makes a claim.

At a meeting, members may begin with dinner, then take turns reading their essays aloud. Each reading is followed by approximately 30 minutes of group discussion, with flexibility to end early or continue when the conversation is especially worthwhile.

The archive creates a living record of a chapter’s thinking. Authors retain their identity and are encouraged to publish essays on their own sites; publication through Junto adds distribution and can link back to those personal publications. The system should eventually support multiple city chapters and could support occasional cross-chapter gatherings.

## Product summary

Junto is a public essay archive and private member portal for essay-based discussion groups.

Members belong to one or more Junto groups. Before each meeting, they write and submit essays through a Markdown editor, associate them with the relevant meeting, and decide whether each essay should be publicly available or visible only to members of their Junto.

Public essays appear in a polished, literary reading experience. Logged-in members can also read private essays, comment on essays, participate in discussion threads, and use a simple group chat shared only with members of their Junto.

The system must support multiple independent Junto groups, even though the first version may launch with only one.
